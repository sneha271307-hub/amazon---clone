What is water conservation?

Water conservation means using freshwater responsibly and cutting down on waste — through everyday habits, better infrastructure, and technology — so supply keeps up with the needs of people, farming, and ecosystems, now and later.

Why it matters

Freshwater is a tiny slice of what's on Earth, and it's unevenly shared. Rising demand, changing rainfall patterns, and ageing infrastructure are putting more regions under water stress — conservation is one of the few levers every household can pull directly.

BY THE NUMBERS

The scale of the problem

3% of all water on Earth is freshwater — most of it locked in glaciers and ice caps. Source: FAO / Concern Worldwide
2.1B people worldwide still lack access to safely managed drinking water at home. Source: WHO / UNICEF, 2025
70% of global freshwater withdrawals go toward agriculture and irrigation. Source: FAO AQUASTAT
2.7B people experience water scarcity for at least one month every year. Source: WWF

WATER SAVING TIPS

Small habits, real savings

Practical, low-cost changes anyone can make at home.

🚰
TIP 01

Turn off taps

Shut the tap while brushing, soaping, or scrubbing dishes instead of letting it run.

🔧
TIP 02

Fix leaks

A slow drip can waste dozens of litres a day — check taps, pipes, and cisterns regularly.

🌧️
TIP 03

Rainwater harvesting

Collect roof runoff in a tank or recharge pit to cut dependence on piped or ground water.

♻️
TIP 04

Reuse RO reject water

Redirect the wastewater from RO purifiers to plants, mopping, or flushing instead of draining it.

🪣
TIP 05

Bucket over shower

A bucket bath typically uses far less water than a long shower — an easy daily swap.

🌳
TIP 06

Plant trees

Tree cover improves groundwater recharge and helps stabilise local rainfall patterns over time.
